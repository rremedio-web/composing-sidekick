# The deck

_Three suits of original cards plus a PERSONAL suit you generate from the owner's own
corpus. All text original._

## SPARK suit (starting)

1. **Mute the loudest opinion.** Whatever instrument leads the loop — silence it
   eight bars and let the second-loudest explain itself.
2. **The mistake is policy now.** Find the note you'd normally fix. Repeat it until
   the track agrees it was intentional.
3. **Start from the room, not the riff.** Sends first: design two bars of pure
   space (reverb/delay throws, no notes), then write the phrase that deserves it.
4. **Steal from your own trash.** Open the rejects track; one of those "dead" rolls is
   tonight's rolling bass. (Quarantined rolls are ostinatos waiting for a context.)
5. **Answer yourself before you write.** Play four notes. Let the sidekick answer
   (any gear). Your next move is to disagree with it — in notes.
6. **Borrow your own hands.** Deal one take-lane improvisation (the raw you) and
   build tonight on its timing, not its pitches.
7. **Ninety seconds, one finger.** One monophonic line, one take, no edit pass.
   Gold it or bury it before you're allowed a second track.

## STRUCTURE suit (the middle)

8. **The transition IS the section.** Take two adjacent MAINs and replace the last
   2 bars of the first with the seam itself — a fill, a cut to silence, a send
   throw. Nothing else changes.
9. **Halve everything once.** Duplicate the loop, delete every second note. If the
   halved version grooves, the full version was upholstery.
10. **Sixteen where eight lives.** Your grid defaults to 8-bar rooms. Knock one
    wall out: one section runs 16 bars with only ONE change inside it. Patience as
    a feature.
11. **The negative B.** Build the B section as A's photographic negative: where A
    is dense, B is air; where A sits low, B floats; same key, one shared motif as
    the umbilical.
12. **Drop the floor, keep the ceiling.** Eight bars with no kick — hats and lead
    carry the pulse alone. (If your kick is law, suspending law is an event.)
13. **Automate one knob like a melody.** Pick cutoff or a send. Write its curve
    FIRST, as the section's actual tune; fit the notes under it. (If your catalogue is
    full of volume rides, you already think this way; do it on purpose.)
14. **Two-bar cells only.** One section built entirely from 2-bar fragments that
    never repeat back-to-back. Your fills become the architecture.

## FINISH suit

15. **Bounce it ugly, now.** Render the arrangement as it stands, tonight, with a
    date in the filename. Perfection is a mixing note, not a composition blocker.
16. **The outro is the intro, played once, thinner.** Copy the intro to the end,
    remove one voice, let it decay. Done beats clever.
17. **Endings audition in threes.** Never argue with one ending — make three
    (decay / subtraction / hard stop into room tone), comp them like takes.
18. **Kill a version tonight.** Open the family (v2/v3/v5/v10…). One becomes the
    song; the others donate organs to the crate and get one-line eulogies. Ten
    maybes are heavier than one no.
19. **Name every section out loud.** If a stretch of timeline can't earn a name,
    it isn't a section — it's a loop wearing a coat. Cut it or promote it.
20. **The last 10% is subtraction.** Your finishing move is rarely adding — remove
    one element from the final chorus/drop that was there the whole track. Absence
    reads as arrival.

## PERSONAL suit (measured — generate these from the owner's corpus)

The public repository ships no PERSONAL cards: they are only true of one composer. Build
ten from live profile queries (`get_style()`, `store/style_profile.json`,
`store/harmony.json`, `store/motifs.json`) and keep the number that makes each card
true next to it, so it can be regenerated. Card shapes that work:

- **Land on the untouched degree.** "`<degree>` is one of your least-touched in-key
  notes (`<share>` of lead notes). End one phrase on it tonight and let it hang
  unresolved for a full bar."
- **Your rarest cell.** "`<cell degrees>` appears in only `<n>` of your tracks. Open with
  it, verbatim, then argue with it for eight bars."
- **The progression you never reach for.** "Your hands go `<common progression>`
  (`<count>` times). Tonight `<rare progression>` instead. Feel where it wants to go next."
- **Cover yourself.** "Take one sketch from your `<era A>` world (`<tempo>`, `<feel>`)
  and perform it as your `<era B>` self: `<tempo>`, `<texture>`. Same bones, other accent."
- **Trust your drag / fight your drag.** "`<share>` of your notes land off-grid, mostly
  `<late|early>`. Record one part pushing the other way — the urgency you almost never use."
- **The shortest breath.** "Your breakdowns halve density; tonight subtract to ONE voice
  for exactly `<n>` bars — the shortest breath you've ever taken on purpose."
- **Your ghost plays first.** "Before writing any lead: deal a take-lane phrase from
  `<years>` ago, transpose nothing, and write only its counterline."
