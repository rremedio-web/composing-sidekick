# Diagnosis — reading a project like a producer (the finisher's procedure)

_For whats_missing, bury-or-marry, version courage, one-hour close. Output is a
diagnosis the owner can ARGUE with: musical words, named bars, no scores-as-verdicts._

## Context to diagnose from (observations, not compulsory shapes)

Read the [shared knowledge guide](../../../../docs/KNOWLEDGE_GUIDE.md), the owner's
current notes and the piece brief plus its direct notes file. Use catalogue statistics
(`structure.json`, the style profile) with their build date and extraction limits; they
describe a varied corpus.

- The corpus's typical lengths, section sizes, dips and descents are comparison
  examples. They do not set a target length, require a break, or cap an ambient opening.
- The owner's stated brief for the current piece outranks every average. Use the
  piece's intended arrivals and returns; do not impose a rise/break/peak/decline
  sequence.
- The roles the corpus favours (a floor, a lead near the median, textural pads) are
  some possible roles. Harmony, register, articulation, timbre and silence can all
  carry the form; a kick is not a required floor in every section.

## The reading order
1. **Frame**: establish sounding duration, versions and the owner's intention. A long
   section is not automatically an unfinished loop; inspect internal transformation,
   automation and the role of sustained repetition.
2. **Sections**: detect boundaries (clip starts/ends, density shifts, automation
   moves). Name what exists in the map vocabulary (INTRO/MAIN/BUILD/DROP/BREATHE/
   REPRISE/OUT). What's present ONCE that should return? What never varies?
3. **Energy line**: trace symbolic activity, register and automation; distinguish these
   from heard impact. Locate proposed arrivals and contrasts without assuming unchanged
   note density means flat energy.
4. **Role coverage**: identify what carries each section and what crosses its edges.
   Use existing owner intent to assess exposed passages; a long solo role or absent beat
   is not automatically a missing part.
5. **The ending**: is the stop or fade an intentional hard stop, a held texture, or
   unfinished? Ask before proposing an ending; sparse or abrupt material can be a
   deliberate choice.
6. **Layering and alternatives**: distinguish composites (paired sounds with identical
   MIDI), archived alternatives, CPU-saving mutes and active experiments. Stacked lanes
   alone establish no verdict.

## Writing the diagnosis
- Lead with a question the owner can answer: "This is a 96-bar A-section; is a
  contrasting B section something you want?" A single section may be intentional.
- Then the ordered missing-piece list, each with a SOURCE STRATEGY:
  - **B section** → negative image of A (invert density/register/rhythm placement,
    keep key + one motif anchor).
  - **Bridge / left-turn** → the take-lane crate or a rare-progression recolour (the
    owner's own rare moves from `idiom.md`).
  - **BREATHE** → subtract: keep one voice + sends open.
  - **Ending** → decay shapes from the owner's finished endings; or "intro once,
    thinner."
- Preserve originals and use candidates/copies within the current authorised workflow.
  Comping and mute changes are observations; log KEEP/DROP only when the owner actually
  gives that judgment.

## Version courage (v2 / v3 / v5 / v10 families)
When the owner wants help choosing a version, identify what is unique in each and
recommend one against their stated musical goal. Do not rank by conventional form
alone, merge version identities, or delete alternatives without authorisation.

## One-hour close discipline
Optional exercise when the owner wants a timebox: concrete micro-moves, each ≤ 10 min:
"commit ending #2" / "fill the bar-64 joint from the crate" / "name the sections" /
"bounce". Agree the useful endpoint from their request; a listening session or an
unjudged experiment is a valid outcome. Do not force a verdict or a bounce to satisfy a
template.
