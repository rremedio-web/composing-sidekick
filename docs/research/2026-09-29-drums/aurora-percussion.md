# Percussion on Ben Frost — *A U R O R A* (2014, Bedroom Community / Mute)

Web research, 2026-09-30, read-only, ~15 min budget. General and reusable; not about any
particular song of ours.

Tags on every claim:
- **[sourced]** — Frost's own words, or a label/venue page (primary).
- **[review]** — a critic's description of what they heard (secondary; descriptive, not authoritative on who played what).
- **[inference]** — my reading of the sourced/review material into note-level moves. Verify by ear.
- **[unverified]** — surfaced in search snippets but no URL confirmed it; do not cite.

## 1. Who played what, and how it was recorded

### 1a. Credits

- Bandcamp (benfrost.bandcamp.com/album/a-u-r-o-r-a) [sourced]: performers are Ben Frost with
  **Shahzad Ismaily, Greg Fox (ex-Liturgy), Thor Harris (Swans)**. *No per-performer instrument and
  no per-track credits on the page.* "Recorded between 2011 and 2013 in Eastern DR Congo, EMPAC New
  York and Reykjavík." Mixed/mastered Valgeir Sigurðsson + Ben Frost, Greenhouse Studios,
  Reykjavík, December 2013. Additional sound design/production: Lawrence English, Tim Hecker.
  Engineers: Daniel Rejmer, Valgeir Sigurðsson, Paul Corley, Paul Evans, Ben Frost.
- Wikipedia [sourced]: no personnel section at all. Discogs returned 403 (not read).
- **Consequence:** every "Harris = bells/chimes, Fox + Ismaily = kit" statement below is [review].
  Reviewers converge on it (DiS, LoBF, Grapevine, Treble) but nobody cites a liner note. If the
  owner has the LP/CD, the physical liner notes are the only primary per-track source.

Reviewer consensus on roles [review]:
- **Thor Harris** — chimes / bells / tubular bells. DiS on *Nolan*: "Harris' trademark chimes
  ringing and echoing around Frost's towering orchestral flourishes." LoBF on *Venter*: "Harris'
  cautionary bell tolls." Grapevine: "hard Teutonic rhythms, chimes and bells produced by Fox and
  Swans' Thor Harris."
- **Greg Fox** — kit, blast/industrial density. DiS on *Nolan*: "Fox and Ismaily add a little blunt
  force trauma." LoBF on *Secant*: "Fox and Harris' clannish beats." RA: Fox, Harris and Ismaily
  "reinforce A U R O R A's computer sheen with real sweat and blood."
- **Shahzad Ismaily** — "longtime live collaborator" (DiS); kit/percussion alongside Fox
  (Grapevine, on the live show: "Greg Fox and Shahzad Ismaily on drums and percussion").
- Live only, not the record [review, Walker Art Center 2014 concert review]: Fox "became something
  akin to a speed metal drummer, sticks and double-bass drum pedals flying"; Fox and Paul Corley
  played "hocketing, polyrhythmic sound, be it on a conventional drum set, congas, toms, or digital
  drums"; "both stood over the congas and toms and struck them with such force that my eyes were
  forced to blink along with every hit"; the closer's "last strains were the most minimal of
  percussive strokes."

### 1b. Frost on how the drummers were used [sourced]

- Quietus (Hyper Realisation, 2014), asked whether the drummers made it aggressive or vice versa:
  *"I knew that they would be involved from day one. I made a series of decisions very early on in
  the process of what would become this record, and they were decisions about the rules relating to
  my way of constructing music."* … *"by even just knowing that at some point Greg would come in and
  work with me on the music, by knowing that at some point in the distant future Thor would be there
  for a few days and we'd work through a few things, it allowed me to make decisions very early on
  about what space could be set aside for them, or built to invite them into."* Also: *"I dispensed
  with a lot of things I considered to be crutches that I would lean on in previous work."*
- Quietus on process: written *"purely 'in the box', not working with anybody"* on a laptop in Congo
  (battery / diesel-generator constrained); the musicians joined *"a lot later on"*; *"the
  foundations of this music found primarily in the digital realm"*; the compositional "DNA" stayed,
  and it became *"quite a struggle"* to reshape.
- Stereogum (Q&A, 2014-05-20; fetch was partial): *"Those are personalities, big, strong, fully
  formed musical personalities. They're not players. These are formidable artists."* … *"The number
  of days it took to make this record versus the number of days Greg or Thor were actually in the
  room is not many… but it's entirely not the point."*
- Vice / Lower Spectrum conversation: *"Getting other people involved in my work is not just about
  the instrument they're playing... it's actually about characters."* … *"there's a spirituality in
  what he does... they're both willing to give themselves over to music and to an experience that
  often involves intense repetition or physical exhaustion."* … *"Endurance and failure were big
  themes in this record, and this shaped the way I worked before they even stepped into the studio."*

Reading [inference]: the drum *slots* were designed first, in the box, as spaces; the humans were
brought in briefly to fill and exceed them; the parts are conceived as endurance (repetition to
exhaustion) rather than as grooves.

### 1c. How the drums were recorded / processed — what is actually sourced

- EMPAC (empac.rpi.edu/events/2013/ben-frost, residency + 2013-02-07 performance) [sourced]:
  *"While using EMPAC's Studio 1 for tracking, he routed sound back through the Concert Hall,
  transforming it into a real-time reverb chamber."* This is the one primary statement about
  room/re-injection. It says "sound", not "drums" specifically.
- Quietus [sourced], on breaking recording rules: *"we're a generation of people who make music with
  screens instead of with ears"*; harmonic distortion on classic records was a *"subtle kind of
  breaking apart of the sonic fabric"* rather than *"pornographic"*.
- Bandcamp blurb [sourced]: *"This is no pristine vision of digital music, it is a filthy,
  uncivilized offering of interrupted future time."*
- Headphone Commute on *Nolan* [review]: *"a repetitive, pummelling rhythm section that is filtered
  and hides behind layers of grime and distortion."* Treble on *Nolan* [review]: *"bass drums
  thumping and bells clanging just beneath the surface."*
- Nothing sourced says "re-amped" or "destroyed". See section 0.4.

## 2. Per-track percussion character

Only two reviewers give any time marker; they are quoted where they exist. Tempos are never stated
in any source read — do not assume one.

**Flex (2:50)** — opener.
- musicOMH [review]: *"the frantic beat that drives the song falters and dies; it's like a musical
  embolism."* Igloo: opens with a *"washing, bowed heartbeat."* DiS: *"joints cracking and spine
  clicking as heavy washes build to an impossible, unsustainable crescendo."* Grapevine: with
  *Nolan*, *"compressed martial trumpets, a pounding barrage of carnage."* Spectrum Culture [review, paraphrased via fetch]: sounds
  indistinguishable from airplane engines during takeoff.
- Peak marking [inference from musicOMH]: the drums do not resolve — they **stop dead** ("falters and
  dies"). Best sourced example on the record of drums-that-stop.

**Nolan (6:58)** — the percussion showpiece.
- DiS [review]: Harris chimes "ringing and echoing," Fox + Ismaily "blunt force trauma"; the track
  takes its germ from the "unsubtle euphorics of house and dance music" and "substitutes nightclub
  communal rites with solitary catharsis." LoBF: *"pounding bass drums and disfigured analog noise."*
  RA: synths *"wrap around the drums like barbed wire."* Treble: *"bass drums thumping and bells
  clanging just beneath the surface."* Spectrum: *"rolling percussion … grows into radioactive
  pulses."* Headphone Commute: rhythm section *"filtered and hides behind layers of grime and
  distortion."* Igloo: *"introduces the bells."* Clash: *"punishing percussion."*
- Character [inference]: four-on-the-floor-derived kick (house "germ") + a second bell/chime layer
  in a different register + kit under a wall that is mixed *over* the drums.

**The Teeth Behind Kisses (3:13)** — aftermath, not a drum study.
- Grapevine [review]: *"views the aftermath, the rhythms passing off into the distance."* Spectrum:
  *"so hollow that they barely seem present."* A snippet says it is "near-silence that gives way to …
  Secant" [unverified URL].

**Secant (4:55)** — the industrial / blast track.
- DiS [review]: *"precision tooled industrial drum blasts next to chiming bells and a flashing swirl
  of tormented feedback"* — "the record's darkest moment." LoBF: *"Fox and Harris' clannish beats
  melding with Frost's flirting, teasing electronics, the track erupts in a firecracker."* Igloo:
  *"marches in steel toed boots"*, whine *"clambers into the forefront with the support of a
  stampeding bass drum."* Headphone Commute: *"Based primarily around a thundering refrain of drums
  (distinctly Rieflin-esque), it explodes halfway through like a proggy supernova, a blast of crispy
  feedback and anthemic synths."* Treble: *"a pulsing, hissing form of industrial."*
- Time markers [review]: Spectrum Culture (paraphrased via fetch) — begins with subtle sounds for
  2.5 minutes, then crescendos; Headphone Commute (verbatim) — *"explodes halfway through."* (Track is 4:55; both agree
  the peak is near the midpoint.)
- Character [inference]: a long low-level intro, then a repeated drum "refrain" (a fixed figure, not
  a groove), bells beside it, peak marked by the wall arriving on top of the drums.

**Diphenyl Oxalate (1:31)** — interlude.
- musicOMH [review]: *"sounds like unfettered nightclub abandon."* Spectrum: once had a melody but
  was *"mauled and eviscerated until only a wall of discord is left."* No drum detail anywhere.

**Venter (6:45)** — the tom / tribal track.
- Grapevine [review]: *"mixes rumbling tom drums with digital pan pipes and arpeggios."* musicOMH:
  *"takes its cue from the unbridled power of the volcano and channels it into a rumbling tribal drum
  affair"*; Harris + Fox turn it into *"a clattering ball of ferocious, yet positive, energy."*
  Tiny Mix Tapes: *"tumbling percussion and high-strung keyboards."* RA: *"hurtles towards you in
  slow motion, sucking in syncopated percussion and tubular bells along the way."* LoBF: *"Harris'
  cautionary bell tolls."* Treble: *"slow ascent from apocalyptic tattoo into claustrophobic death
  rave. It's almost a dance track."* Spectrum: *"brutal drum and tumbling bells work evolving into a
  harrowing and dangerous beast."* Igloo: *"Riding a crispy, Tool-esque spattering thrum, it delivers
  again the bells, and again the whine, but something is more locked into itself."*
- Time marker [review, quoted, Igloo]: *"when the beat drops halfway and stays with you, you're ready
  for it. A satisfying whirlwind ensues, and then suddenly subsides."*
- Character [inference]: tom-led, syncopated, slow-feel, "tattoo" (military drum figure) that
  accelerates in density rather than tempo; bells toll against it; beat drop near the midpoint; ends
  by subsiding, not stopping.

**No Sorrowing (4:27)** — sparse.
- Spectrum [review, paraphrased via fetch]: extremely sparse opening of whispered textures before
  building intensity. musicOMH: *"finds a way to make engine roar calming."* No drum detail.

**Sola Fide (6:27)** — thin evidence.
- Igloo [review]: *"clearly the highlight"*; *"Robots clap hands according to forgotten algorithms."*
  A snippet says it *"flirts briefly with drum and bass patterns"* [unverified URL]. Nothing else read
  names its drums. Listen candidate, not a confirmed drum study.

**A Single Point of Blinding Light (3:18)** — closer.
- DiS [review]: *"thunderously chaotic percussion"* under *"hard edged glow in the dark synth
  flashes."* Spectrum: *"rushes into a surprisingly danceable beat with pulsating and uneasy synths
  mingling with crashing drums."* Headphone Commute: *"a furious tumble of beats and synths, fighting
  each other in time with a frantic desperation."* Grapevine: *"a temple throbbing collapsing of
  rhythms."*

## 3. Techniques worth borrowing at note level (MIDI drum part) — all [inference]

Each is derived from a sourced descriptor; none is a transcription. Verify by ear on the named track.

1. **Two players layered by role, not doubled.** (DiS on *Nolan*: chimes "ringing and echoing" +
   "blunt force trauma".) One lane = low kit hitting on a fixed, house-derived pulse; second lane =
   metallic/bell layer on long, irregular, ringing notes that do not lock to the kick grid. Give the
   bell lane sustain (long note lengths, little damping) and place its hits off the kick's strong
   beats so the two are audibly different agents.
2. **Tom-led "tattoo" instead of kick/snare.** (Grapevine/musicOMH/Treble on *Venter*: "rumbling tom
   drums", "tribal", "apocalyptic tattoo".) Build the figure on 2–3 toms with a short repeating cell
   (a military-style roll-and-accent), syncopated; keep snare absent or as a rare accent; raise
   density (add 16ths/flams) over bars rather than raising tempo — reviewers hear "slow motion" and
   "slow ascent" while the energy climbs.
3. **Drum "refrain," not groove.** (Headphone Commute on *Secant*: "thundering refrain of drums".)
   A fixed multi-bar figure repeated verbatim as a hook, treated as a riff. Quantized, low velocity
   variation — the "precision tooled industrial" (DiS) feel comes from sameness, not humanization.
4. **Blast / roll texture under the wall.** (DiS on *Secant*: "industrial drum blasts"; Walker live:
   double-bass pedals.) Continuous 16th/32nd kick or snare stream at nearly constant velocity as a
   *texture layer*, mixed under (not over) the synth wall; it is a bed, not a fill.
5. **Bell tolls as structural markers.** (LoBF on *Venter*: "cautionary bell tolls"; RA: "tubular
   bells".) Very sparse single low bell notes, long, repeated at a period unrelated to the bar (e.g.
   every 5 or 7 beats), announcing sections; not a melody.
6. **Drums buried by the wall at the peak.** (Headphone Commute on *Nolan*: "filtered and hides behind
   layers of grime and distortion"; Treble: "just beneath the surface"; RA: synths "wrap around the
   drums like barbed wire".) Peak = do not add drum hits; keep the pattern constant and let the
   harmonic/noise layers swallow it. In MIDI terms: hold the pattern, cut drum bus level/HF, raise
   everything else.
7. **Long silence, then midpoint explosion.** (Spectrum on *Secant*: "subtle sounds for 2.5 minutes";
   Headphone Commute: "explodes halfway through"; Igloo on *Venter*: "beat drops halfway".) Enter the
   full drum figure near the midpoint of the piece, not at bar 1; the long empty lead-in is the
   design.
8. **Drums that stop dead.** (musicOMH on *Flex*: "the frantic beat … falters and dies; it's like a
   musical embolism".) End a section by truncating the pattern mid-figure — no fill, no ritardando,
   one or two straggling hits ("falters") then nothing. Contrast with *Venter*, which "suddenly
   subsides" (fade of density, not a cut).
9. **Endurance as the part's shape.** (Frost, Vice: "intense repetition or physical exhaustion";
   "Endurance and failure were big themes".) Write the part longer than comfortable and let it
   degrade (dropped hits, velocity sag) late, rather than evolve; the failure is the arc.

## 4. Frost's own words on rhythm on this record [sourced]

- Stereogum (2014-05-20), on dance music / rhythm / euphoria: *"I'm sure there's no surprise to
  anyone that the bass drum is an externalization of the heart. That's why we're drawn to that idea.
  That's why we've pulled towards rhythm. That's why house music is so fucking popular! It sits at
  the right tempo."* … *"It's not something that we're inventing out of nowhere. It comes from
  somewhere so much deeper, so much older."*
- Stereogum: *"Those are personalities, big, strong, fully formed musical personalities. They're not
  players. These are formidable artists."*
- Quietus: *"…what space could be set aside for them, or built to invite them into."* /
  *"I dispensed with a lot of things I considered to be crutches…"*
- Vice / Lower Spectrum: *"Endurance and failure were big themes in this record."* /
  *"…intense repetition or physical exhaustion."*
- Guardian review: not fetchable (blocked). Pitchfork review (8.5): not fetchable; via search snippet
  only, the one line surfaced is *"A U R O R A can be heard as Frost's attempt to create something physical, and it
  stands above the rest of his discography."*

## 5. Listen list (for a verified `analyze_audio_ref` pass — owner picks `focus`)

Ordered by how much sourced drum evidence exists:

1. **Nolan** — two-role layering (kick pulse + Harris chimes), drums buried by the wall.
2. **Venter** — tom-led tribal/tattoo, bell tolls, midpoint beat drop, subsides at the end.
3. **Secant** — long quiet lead-in (~2.5 min per Spectrum, paraphrased), drum refrain, blast/industrial, explodes at
   the midpoint.
4. **Flex** — the beat that "falters and dies" (stop-dead study; short, 2:50).
5. **A Single Point of Blinding Light** — "chaotic"/"crashing" drums against a danceable beat.
6. **Sola Fide** — thin evidence ("robots clap hands"; d'n'b hint unverified); listen to decide.

Not drum studies per the reviews read: *The Teeth Behind Kisses* (aftermath), *Diphenyl Oxalate*
(1:31 interlude), *No Sorrowing* (sparse).
